03 / Biography

A line through Mozart

From Växjö to Salzburg, then the Frankfurt ensemble — and a first summer on the festival circuit.

Karolina Bengtsson grew up in Växjö, in Småland, and trained first at the Royal Danish Academy of Music in Copenhagen, then at the Mozarteum in Salzburg. There Barbara Bonney guided a master’s degree across opera, Lied and oratorio; Wolfgang Holzmair worked with her on song, Gaiva Bandzinaite on role preparation. In 2021 she sang her first Pamina on the university stage — a role that has since followed her into the house.

Oper Frankfurt took her into the Opera Studio in 2021/22 (Clotilde, Frasquita, the Second Woman in Dido and Aeneas, Suor Osmina). The following season brought Papagena and Pamina in Ted Huffman’s new Zauberflöte, Isaura in Mercadante’s Francesca da Rimini — also at the Tiroler Festspiele Erl — and Kate Pinkerton. Since 2023/24 she has been a member of the soloist ensemble.

The ensemble years have been unusually dense: Silvia in Ascanio in Alba, Belisa in Fortner’s Don Perlimplín, a Young Shepherd in Tannhäuser, the First Maid in Strauss’s Daphne, then a run of role debuts — Barbarina (including a last-minute appearance at the Royal Swedish Opera), Aljeja in Janáček’s From the House of the Dead, a Lady-in-Waiting in Macbeth, Sophie in Werther, Anna in Vito Žuraj’s Blühen, and Fiordiligi. Summer 2025 opened two festival doors: Camille in Charpentier’s Louise at Aix-en-Provence, and Dori in Traetta’s Ifigenia in Tauride at the Innsbrucker Festwochen der Alten Musik.

Concert work sits beside the theatre. She has appeared with the Swedish Radio Symphony Orchestra and the Gothenburg Symphony Orchestra, and her oratorio shelf includes Haydn’s Die Schöpfung, Brahms’s Ein deutsches Requiem, Bach’s St Matthew Passion, Pergolesi’s Stabat Mater and Lars-Erik Larsson’s Förklädd Gud. In 2018 Daniel Nelson wrote Döden och Julia for her; she premiered and later recorded it with Malin Broman and Musica Vitae.

A lyrically clear and finely tuned soprano — from shouting with joy to dying of sorrow.
